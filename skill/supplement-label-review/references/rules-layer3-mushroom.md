# Layer 3 — Category Module: MUSHROOM / FUNGAL SUPPLEMENTS

**Gate:** any dietary ingredient is a fungus (mushroom fruiting body, mycelium, sclerotium, or fungal extract).

This module encodes category-specific traps that generic label review misses. It supplements, never replaces, Layers 1 and 2.

---

## MSH1. ⭐ Import Alert 25-05 — powder vs. extract
**Authority:** FDA Import Alert 25-05, "Detention Without Physical Examination of Dried Fungus (Mushrooms) from Hong Kong and PROC for Filth"
**Background:** Issued after 20 of 22 samples (91%) of dried fungus from Hong Kong and the PRC were detained for filth between October 1981 and March 1982. It remains active.

**Scope, precisely:**
- **Subject to DWPE:** dried fungi, dried mushrooms, **mushroom powder**, and **pulverized/ground mushrooms** from Hong Kong and the PRC.
- **Excluded from DWPE:** **mushroom/fungi extracts** — "as those products undergo further processing that would reduce or remove filth."
- Firms on the alert's Green List are exempt.

**Why this is a labelling rule:** DWPE is applied on the basis of how the entry is described and how the product presents. A product that is genuinely an extract but labelled "Mushroom Powder" invites detention; a product labelled "Extract Powder" that is in fact milled dried mushroom is a misbranding problem in the other direction.

**Evaluate:**
1. Is the country of origin Hong Kong or the PRC?
2. Does the statement of identity say "powder", "extract", or "extract powder"?
3. Does the Supplement Facts panel say "extract" for each fungal ingredient?
4. Is the PDP wording consistent with the panel wording?

**Verdict logic:**
- PASS — origin is HK/PRC, product is a true extract, and both PDP and panel say "extract" consistently.
- **FAIL (misbranding risk)** — panel says "extract" but PDP says only "Mushroom Powder", or vice versa. Inconsistency is the failure.
- **FLAG (import risk, not a labelling violation)** — origin is HK/PRC and the product is a true non-extract powder. The label may be perfectly lawful; the shipment is simply exposed to DWPE. Report as a commercial risk, clearly separated from compliance findings.
- N/A — origin is not HK/PRC.

**Correction template:**
> Align the PDP and Supplement Facts wording. For a true extract: `{Mushroom} Extract Powder` on the PDP and `{Common} ({Latin}) {part} extract` in the panel. Note: Import Alert 25-05 subjects mushroom *powder* from HK/PRC to detention without physical examination, while *extracts* are excluded — so the distinction carries commercial as well as regulatory weight.

⚠️ Do not overstate this. The Import Alert is not a labelling regulation, and a truthful "powder" label is not unlawful. Present it as risk information, and never let it pressure a label into describing a product as something it is not — that would be misbranding under §403(a)(1).

## MSH2. ⭐ Fungal part terminology — the part must be anatomically correct
**Citation:** 21 CFR 101.4(h)(1) via 101.36(d)(1) (see Layer 1 rule E1)
**Rule:** The declared part must be the actual anatomical structure used. "Fruiting body" is not a universal default.

**Reference table — correct part terms:**

| Common name | Latin binomial | Correct part term(s) |
|---|---|---|
| Reishi | *Ganoderma lucidum* | fruiting body |
| Lion's Mane | *Hericium erinaceus* | fruiting body |
| Chaga | *Inonotus obliquus* | **sclerotium** (the harvested conk is a sterile sclerotial mass, not a fruiting body) |
| Poria / Fu Ling | *Wolfiporia extensa* (syn. *Poria cocos*) | **sclerotium** |
| Cordyceps militaris | *Cordyceps militaris* | fruiting body |
| Cordyceps sinensis | *Ophiocordyceps sinensis* | stroma with larva — **almost never the actual ingredient**; see MSH4 |
| Maitake | *Grifola frondosa* | fruiting body |
| Turkey Tail | *Trametes versicolor* | fruiting body |
| Shiitake | *Lentinula edodes* | fruiting body |
| Agaricus blazei | *Agaricus subrufescens* | fruiting body |
| Tremella | *Tremella fuciformis* | fruiting body |
| Any mycelial product | — | **mycelium** (and see MSH3 on substrate) |

**Verdict logic:**
- PASS — every fungal ingredient names an anatomically correct part.
- FAIL — a sclerotial species (Chaga, Poria) is labelled "fruiting body"; or a mycelial product is labelled "fruiting body".
- NEEDS-INFO — the part actually used is unknown.

⚠️ **Chaga is the most-missed case.** Even careful reviewers catch Poria and miss Chaga. What is harvested from the birch is a sterile sclerotial conk; the true fruiting body forms only after the host tree dies and is not what enters commerce. Labelling Chaga as "fruiting body" is inaccurate.

⚠️ **Taxonomic note on Poria:** the currently accepted binomial is *Wolfiporia extensa*. *Poria cocos* is a widely used synonym and remains common in commerce and in AHPA's *Herbs of Commerce*. Either is defensible; flag it as an advisory rather than a failure, and prefer the accepted name for new artwork.

## MSH3. ⭐ Mycelium-on-grain and the "no grain / no starch / no fillers" conflict
**Citation:** FD&C Act §403(a)(1) (misbranding); 21 CFR 101.4(a)(1) (all ingredients declared)
**Background:** Most low-cost US-produced "mushroom" material is mycelium grown on a sterilised grain substrate (typically rice or oats) and harvested together with that substrate — sold as **myceliated grain**. The grain is not separable and can constitute the majority of the finished mass.

**The conflicts:**
1. If the product contains myceliated grain, the grain is an ingredient and must be declared.
2. A "no grain", "no starch", or "no fillers" claim is false on a myceliated-grain product.
3. Declaring the ingredient as "*Hericium erinaceus* fruiting body" when it is myceliated grain fails E1 and MSH2 and is misbranding.
4. β-glucan figures on myceliated grain frequently reflect **cereal** β-glucan from the substrate, not fungal β-glucan — see MSH5.

**Evaluate:** Cross-check the ingredient declaration, any "no grain/starch/filler" claims, and the stated part.

**Verdict logic:**
- PASS — product is fruiting-body derived, or is myceliated grain with the substrate properly declared and no conflicting free-from claims.
- FAIL — "no grain"/"no starch"/"no fillers" claim on a myceliated-grain product.
- FAIL — myceliated grain declared as "fruiting body".
- NEEDS-INFO (**default**) — production method not determinable from the label. Ask the supplier directly.

**Question to put to the user:**
> Is this material fruiting-body derived, or mycelium grown on grain substrate? If myceliated grain, the substrate must be declared and any "no grain / no starch / no fillers" claim must be removed.

## MSH4. Cordyceps species identity
**Citation:** 21 CFR 101.4(h)(2); FD&C Act §403(a)(1)
**Rule:** Wild *Ophiocordyceps sinensis* is among the most expensive biological commodities on earth and is effectively absent from mass-market supplements. Commercial "cordyceps" is almost always cultivated *Cordyceps militaris*, or a *Cordyceps* mycelial biomass (historically the CS-4 strain).

**Verdict logic:**
- PASS — binomial matches the material actually used.
- FAIL — labelled *Cordyceps sinensis* / *Ophiocordyceps sinensis* while supplying *C. militaris* or mycelial biomass.
- NEEDS-INFO — species not stated, or supplier documentation not reviewed.

⚠️ Imagery counts. Depicting the wild caterpillar-fungus form on a product containing cultivated *C. militaris* is misleading labelling under §403(a)(1) even if the binomial is technically correct.

## MSH5. Beta-glucan claims — method specificity
**Citation:** FD&C Act §403(a)(1); 21 CFR 111 subpart E
**Rule:** A stated β-glucan percentage requires a method that measures **fungal** β-glucan specifically.

**The problem:** generic "polysaccharide" assays measure total polysaccharide and cannot distinguish β-glucan from α-glucan. Substrate starch is α-glucan. A myceliated-grain product can therefore return an impressive "polysaccharide" figure that contains very little fungal β-glucan. Cereal β-glucan kits (developed for oats and barley) have the same blind spot in reverse.

**Evaluate:** For any β-glucan or polysaccharide claim, request the COA and the test method.

**Verdict logic:**
- NEEDS-INFO (**default**) — request method and COA.
- FAIL — claim stated as β-glucan but the COA measures total polysaccharide.

**Question to put to the user:**
> Which assay produced the {claimed}% figure — a fungal-specific enzymatic β-glucan method, a cereal β-glucan kit, or a total-polysaccharide method? Only the first supports a fungal β-glucan claim.

⚠️ Also confirm the figure is stated on the correct basis (as-is vs. dry weight) and matches the finished product, not the raw material.

## MSH6. "100% Fruiting Body" as a composition claim
**Citation:** FD&C Act §403(a)(1); and note the interaction with Layer 1 rule E1
**Rule:** Two separate requirements are often conflated:
1. **E1 (mandatory):** the plant/fungal part must appear **inside the Supplement Facts panel** as part of the ingredient name.
2. **MSH6 (this rule):** a "100% Fruiting Body" badge elsewhere on the pack is a voluntary composition claim requiring substantiation.

Satisfying one does not satisfy the other. A pack can carry the badge and still fail E1; a pack can satisfy E1 and still lack substantiation for the badge.

**Verdict logic:**
- PASS — badge substantiated by supplier documentation **and** the part declared inside the panel.
- FAIL — badge present but the panel omits the part (this is an E1 failure; cite both).
- NEEDS-INFO — substantiation not reviewed.

## MSH7. Extract ratio and concentration disclosure
**Citation:** 21 CFR 101.36(b)(3)(ii)(B), (C)
**Rule:** See Layer 1 rule D10. For fungal extracts specifically, a stated ratio ("8:1", "10:1") must reflect the actual starting-material-to-extract relationship, and the declared mg must be the **extract** weight, not the raw-equivalent.

**Verdict logic:**
- NEEDS-INFO (default).
- FAIL — the amount column declares a raw-material equivalent rather than the extract weight.

⚠️ A common pattern: label declares "1000 mg (equivalent to 8000 mg mushroom)" in the amount column. Under 101.36(b)(3)(ii), the amount column must carry the extract weight; the equivalence may be stated parenthetically, not substituted for it.

## MSH8. Heavy metals — Chaga, Reishi, and cultivated species
**Citation:** 21 CFR 111 subpart E; FD&C Act §402 (adulteration); California Proposition 65 (state)
**Rule:** Not a federal labelling requirement, but a material commercial risk. Fungi are efficient bioaccumulators. Chaga in particular concentrates metals from its host and substrate; wild-harvested material varies widely by site.

**Verdict logic:** NEEDS-INFO (advisory). Recommend heavy-metal COAs (lead, arsenic, cadmium, mercury) per lot.

⚠️ **California Proposition 65** applies to product sold into California and can require a warning at exposure levels well below FDA action levels — lead in particular. If the user sells DTC nationally, this is live. Flag it; do not treat it as an FDA finding.

## MSH9. Chaga and oxalate
**Citation:** advisory only
**Rule:** Chaga is notably high in oxalates, and case reports link sustained high-dose consumption to oxalate nephropathy. No FDA warning is mandated.

**Verdict logic:** ADVISORY. If the label promotes high-dose or continuous daily Chaga use, note the consideration. Do not manufacture a warning statement that implies a disease claim — a caution about kidney conditions can itself edge into 101.93(g) territory. Keep any language general and consult counsel.

## MSH10. Fungal allergen considerations
**Citation:** FD&C Act §403(w); 21 CFR 101.4
**Rule:** Fungi are not among the nine major allergens, so no FALCPA declaration is triggered by the mushroom itself. However:
- Myceliated **grain** substrate can introduce **wheat** — a major allergen.
- Shared facilities and carriers can introduce others.

**Verdict logic:**
- NEEDS-INFO — confirm substrate identity and facility allergen controls.
- FAIL — wheat-based substrate present without a wheat declaration.

⚠️ This is the intersection of MSH3 and Layer 1 rule H1, and it is the most consequential failure in the whole mushroom module: an undeclared wheat allergen is a recall-class event, not a paperwork issue.

## MSH11. Species-specific structure/function claim risk
**Citation:** 21 CFR 101.93(g)
**Rule:** Certain mushroom claims sit close to the disease-claim line. Screen these patterns with particular care:

| Risky phrasing | Why | Safer direction |
|---|---|---|
| "boosts immunity" / "fights infection" | implies effect on disease — (g)(2)(i), (viii) | "supports healthy immune function" |
| "improves memory" / "prevents cognitive decline" | cognitive decline is a disease state — (g)(2)(i) | "supports cognitive function" |
| "reduces inflammation" | inflammation as pathology — (g)(2)(ii) | "supports a healthy inflammatory response" |
| "anti-tumor" / "anti-cancer" | explicit disease claim | not usable |
| "lowers blood sugar" / "lowers cholesterol" | targets diagnostic markers — (g)(2)(ii) | "supports healthy glucose metabolism already in the normal range" |
| "adaptogen" | not an FDA-recognised category; acceptable only with a structure/function frame | "traditionally used to help the body adapt to stress" |
| "detox" / "cleanse" | implies treating a pathological state | avoid |

**Verdict logic:** FAIL for any claim meeting a (g)(2) criterion; cite the specific criterion.

⚠️ **Also screen the imagery** under 101.93(g)(2)(iv)(E) — see Layer 1 rule F4. Brain graphics beside a Lion's Mane claim, or immune-cell/virus imagery beside an immunity claim, can create a disease claim on their own.

## MSH12. Traditional-use framing
**Citation:** 21 CFR 101.93(g)(2)(iv)(C)
**Rule:** Historical or traditional-use statements ("used in Traditional Chinese Medicine for centuries") are permissible framing, but become disease claims if the tradition cited is a disease treatment tradition and the context implies the product treats that disease.

**Regulation says (g)(2)(iv)(C):** a disease claim arises through "citation of a publication or reference, if the citation refers to a disease use, and if, in the context of the labeling as a whole, the citation implies treatment or prevention of a disease, e.g., through placement on the immediate product label or packaging, inappropriate prominence, or lack of relationship to the product's express claims."

**Verdict logic:**
- PASS — traditional-use language is general and does not name a disease or condition.
- FAIL — traditional use is tied to a specific disease.
- NEEDS-INFO — borderline; refer to counsel.
